 ## Research Experience
Reserch project— Tel Aviv University
During my undergraduate studies, I worked on a research project investigating the MazEF toxin–antitoxin system in Bacillus subtilis. We examined whether dimerization of the MazE antitoxin is essential for neutralizing the MazF toxin, and explored the conservation of this system by studying the MazEF system from Listeria. Through this project, I gained hands-on experience with bacterial growth assays, phage infection and plaque assays, and FACS.

Research Project — Weizmann Institute of Science
I worked on an experimental evolution project investigating cold adaptation in Saccharomyces cerevisiae. We examined whether independently evolved yeast lines showed improved growth under cold conditions and explored the relationship between short-term physiological responses and long-term genetic adaptation. Through this project, I gained experience in analyzing growth curve parameters, fitting mathematical models to experimental data, writing code, and analyzing biological data.

## Current Research — Dr. Tanita Wein’s Lab, Weizmann Institute of Science
I am currently studying defense systems in bacteria from different species and their connections to immune systems in other organisms, including humans. My research focuses on gasdermin, a protein conserved from bacteria to humans. In bacteria, gasdermin can be activated during a bacteriophage infection and trigger a form of programmed cell death, helping protect the bacterial population by limiting the spread of the virus.
